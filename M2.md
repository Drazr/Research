# M2: cross-language transfer methodology for G9-Q6 and G7

Prepared 14 November 2026. Companion to [M1.md](M1.md), [research.md](research.md) and [paper.md](paper.md).

This file screens **all 62 category-2 papers**. It contains **14 core references, 22 potentially useful supporting references and 26 background/not-shortlisted references**. The first reading pack has eight core references; the rest address particular needs. These are method-selection dispositions, not paper deletions, required implementations or preliminary experiments.

**T01-T62 are local M2 IDs**, following category-2 catalog order. **S01-S53 refer to M1**; they are not the same numbering system. Original methods were assessed from their primary texts before identifying connections to M1. Each core card separates the published method, its prerequisites, our bounded adaptation and its relation to M1. A method can challenge M1's assumptions or add a different route; it is not retained only because it agrees with M1.

All 62 primary full texts were available, including reused primary-source texts from the earlier benchmark review. Abstracts/scope were screened across the set, with targeted method and limitation reading for the shortlisted contributions. This is not a line-by-line review of every appendix or an exhaustive novelty check. Source links identify the papers; proposed category-readout adaptations are our inferences and are not attributed to the original authors.

## 1. Question contract and role of M1

### G9. How are category discrimination, cross-language readout compatibility and confidence reliability related under released caption contexts?

Relate category discrimination, cross-context readout compatibility and confidence reliability. Similarity, ranking quality and calibration are different measurements.

### Q6. Can cross-language readout incompatibility coexist with accessible image-category information under the released native-caption contexts?

Distinguish category accessibility within a context from the portability of another language/context's readout. A coordinate explanation is one possibility, not an established mechanism.

### G7. Can joint reporting of the released categories become unreliable while individual category information remains accessible?

Relate accessible category information to a structured report over the released root-category labels. Joint category reporting is not object counting or native-language generation quality.

M1 already supplies frozen probes, capacity controls, stage/position comparisons, subspace diagnostics and confidence definitions. M2 adds mapping/normalization objectives, teacher-reference choices, transfer-setting distinctions and retrieval/reporting ingredients. **Neither file turns geometric agreement into proof of causal model use.** Standby questions are not reactivated by a source-selection or cue-related reference in this file.

## 2. No-new-manual-validation or dataset-work boundary

- **Semantic data remain unchanged:** the existing XM3600 images, original English/Hindi/Bengali/Telugu captions, image/language metadata and the shared 25-category CSV. There is no new native-speaker review, translation checking, visual adjudication, free-caption assertion labeling, native category-name inventory or additional semantic dataset.
- **Existing release labels remain fallible targets:** invalid/nonbinary cells remain unknown under a deterministic mask; they are not human-repaired. Counts, relations, OCR regions, cultural identity and fine subtypes are not inferred from root presence. M1's rare-category and image-group limitations still apply.
- **Automatic computation is permitted:** feature extraction, joins, numerical development selection, readout fitting, feature maps, training-only reference indexing and fixed-ID scoring. A new algorithmic organization of released records is not new human annotation. “Validation” here means numerical development evaluation, never manual semantic verification.
- **Native captions are not translations:** image identity gives visual co-reference and shared category truth, not sentence equivalence. Using image-matched views to fit a map is image-pair supervision, not dictionary-free discovery of native semantic equivalence. New translations, native lexical mappings or meaning-controlled paraphrases are not silently added.
- **The base VLM remains frozen for the bounded methods:** fitting diagnostic projections/readouts is permitted; activation patching, steering, erasure, layer reinitialization, new backbone training and adapter fine-tuning are not requirements. Papers using such methods remain valuable sources of hypotheses, but their complete training procedures are not promised as annotation-free executable recipes here.
- **G7 retains a common formal interface:** category identifiers must be associated explicitly with the release's categories; an opaque ID has no natural semantic meaning. Structured correctness comes from existing known cells. Format failure and category error remain distinct, with coverage and denominators declared. This is not verified native instruction comprehension or native caption factuality.
- **Model outputs are not new gold:** teacher scores, induced neighbors, retrieved concepts and generated captions can be inputs or diagnostic estimates, but not independently verified semantic labels. Target query labels cannot be used as retrieved guidance or an oracle prompt.
- **All four languages remain eligible:** ongoing presence/reporting questions use original captions and image labels, not the unreliable Bengali/Telugu derived mention files. No mention extraction is needed for M2's ongoing scope.

Pretrained feature extractors can be reused as computational components if available; that does not make their predictions ground truth. A source method's external parallel corpus, private training data, lexical database, human-audited translations or task benchmark is not assumed available merely because the paper reports an annotation-free step.

## 4. Core method cards

“Bounded adaptation” means a possible methodological ingredient consistent with the stated data contract. It is not a literal reproduction when the original objective or supervision has changed. These cards are not an experiment grid, prompt specification or competing-explanation test plan.

### T02. [Offline bilingual word vectors, orthogonal transformations and the inverted softmax](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03859) (2017)

**Priority:** Start here. **Question links:** Q6; G9.

**Method location:** Orthogonal alignment and inverted-softmax retrieval. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.03859).

**Published method:** Align pretrained language spaces by an SVD-based orthogonal transformation; use inverted softmax to reduce asymmetric nearest-neighbor hubness. The original work uses lexical or sentence correspondences.

**Original prerequisites:** Corresponding anchors and sufficiently compatible spaces; retrieval also needs a defined candidate distribution.

**Bounded adaptation:** Existing same-image caption states can provide weak visual anchors for a post-extraction diagnostic map. They are not translation-equivalent anchors. No word dictionary is constructed.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Extends S34's constrained geometry and S39's functional/geometric distinction with an explicit coordinate-map alternative. S01/S44 retain the capacity reference.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Fit anchors and preprocessing only on training-image groups; keep map direction and rank explicit. Compare centering, rotation and readout fitting as separate operations.

**Limits / do not import:** A rotation fitted to independent caption contexts may reflect content selection as well as coordinates. Inverted-softmax retrieval is not a calibrated category probability. Its supervision and tuning must be declared; no pure language-cause inference follows.

### T04. [Learning principled bilingual mappings of word embeddings while preserving monolingual invariance](https://aclanthology.org/D16-1250/) (2016)

**Priority:** Start here. **Question links:** Q6; G9.

**Method location:** Section 2: mapping constraints, length normalization and mean centering. [Primary full text](https://aclanthology.org/D16-1250.pdf).

**Published method:** Derive exact bilingual mapping solutions under constraints that preserve monolingual dot-product structure; analyze normalization and centering in that mapping objective.

**Original prerequisites:** Comparable anchor embeddings and a declared geometry-preservation objective.

**Bounded adaptation:** Use the geometric constraints as alternative readout preprocessors on existing features, with common-image anchors or released category supervision where explicitly allowed.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Adds choices of mapping objective to S34, while S17/S39 supply subspace/direction diagnostics. It challenges an assumption that any fitted linear map measures the same accessibility property.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Distinguish a restrictive rotation from an unconstrained linear predictor. Match capacity and training support; define whether means/norms come from source or target training views.

**Limits / do not import:** The original dictionary and analogy evaluation are not supplied. Norm preservation does not prove preservation of native meaning. A high-dimensional map with too few independent anchors can be underdetermined even when an SVD solution exists.

### T14. [Cross-lingual Alignment Methods for Multilingual BERT: A Comparative Study](https://aclanthology.org/2020.findings-emnlp.83/) (2020)

**Priority:** Start here. **Question links:** Q6 primary; G9.

**Method location:** Sections 2.1 and 4: language centering, rotation and supervision comparisons. [Primary full text](https://aclanthology.org/2020.findings-emnlp.83.pdf).

**Published method:** Compare dictionary versus parallel-corpus supervision and rotational versus model-fine-tuning alignment. Center language distributions before rotation and restore the destination mean when mapping features.

**Original prerequisites:** Original experiments use contextual word alignment and external parallel/dictionary resources, which are not assumed available here.

**Bounded adaptation:** Retain centering/offset handling and constrained post-extraction maps using released image groups. A language-context mean is an estimated distribution statistic, not a verified language-only offset.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Complements S34/S39 by separating translation offsets from directional agreement; S29 guards against overinterpreting the resulting geometry.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Document means, feature basis, anchor construction, map direction and fitting images. Same-image captions need not assert the same content; word-level alignments cannot be inferred from image IDs.

**Limits / do not import:** No fine-tuning, dictionary creation or translation verification is imported. Improved transfer after centering does not uniquely identify language interference or restore known semantic equivalence.

### T18. [Making Monolingual Sentence Embeddings Multilingual using Knowledge Distillation](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.365/) (2020)

**Priority:** Start here. **Question links:** Q6; G9.

**Method location:** Section 2: teacher/student MSE training on parallel sentences. [Primary full text](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.365.pdf).

**Published method:** Distill a monolingual sentence space into a multilingual student by mapping original and translated sentences to the teacher's original embedding.

**Original prerequisites:** Sentence translations are the original semantic correspondence assumption; ordinary XM3600 captions do not satisfy it.

**Bounded adaptation:** A limited readout-level analogue can share category supervision or imitate declared category scores across training views of an image. This preserves a common label target, not all caption semantics; base encoders remain frozen.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Adds teacher/target choices to S01/S34 and a challenge to S39: matching English features may copy source-specific content. S33 requires separate reliability assessment.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Separate hard CSV labels from teacher-produced soft targets; isolate teacher/student fitting and final images. Name target-view or paired-image supervision explicitly.

**Limits / do not import:** An English teacher is not ground truth and its probabilities need not be calibrated. Unchanged source performance or reduced feature distance does not certify preserved native descriptive choices. The original model-training procedure is not reproduced.

### T27. [IGLUE: A Benchmark for Transfer Learning across Modalities, Tasks, and Languages](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/bugliarello22a.html) (2022)

**Priority:** Start here. **Question links:** G9; Q6; G7 task interpretation.

**Method location:** Zero-shot, few-shot and translate-test evaluation settings. [Primary full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/bugliarello22a/bugliarello22a.pdf).

**Published method:** Standardize multilingual visual-language transfer evaluation across several tasks, separating zero-shot transfer, labeled target adaptation and translation-based testing.

**Original prerequisites:** Its original task-specific gold and translated tests are not part of the selected collection.

**Bounded adaptation:** Use its setting distinctions to label category-readout evaluations honestly. Existing shared presence labels enable target-context comparators without new human work, but fitting them is still target supervision.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Clarifies the fitting boundaries of S31/S33/S39 and prevents a supervised map or calibration step from being described as unchanged source transfer.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Keep image identities disjoint and report exactly which source/target inputs, labels and examples influence fitting or selection. Separate task difficulty from transfer penalty.

**Limits / do not import:** Do not import translation-test claims, human annotation requirements or few-shot gains as expected results. The study itself finds task-dependent difficulty in exploiting target examples. Shared images are not controlled translations or a universal task-equivalence guarantee.

### T40. [First Align, then Predict: Understanding the Cross-Lingual Ability of Multilingual BERT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11109) (2021)

**Priority:** Start here. **Question links:** Q6; G9; G7 interpretation.

**Method location:** Section 2: RANDOM-INIT and layer-wise hidden-state similarity. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.11109).

**Published method:** Study multilingual alignment versus task prediction using layer reinitialization followed by fine-tuning, together with CKA of pooled parallel-sentence representations.

**Original prerequisites:** Its causal/behavioral argument uses changed model parameters and annotated downstream tasks; similarity uses parallel sentences.

**Bounded adaptation:** Retain the conceptual separation and observational layer profiles using released category targets. No reinitialization or base-model updating is included.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Connects S24/S05's site comparisons with S31's layer-transfer distinction; S39/S29 caution that similarity alone is not useful transfer or native causal computation.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Pair similarity rows by known image identity, name the independent-caption confound, and measure held-out category transfer separately. Do not equate a high-CKA layer with the model's alignment mechanism.

**Limits / do not import:** Our observational adaptation cannot reproduce the original intervention-supported localization. The paper's lower/upper-layer division in mBERT is a hypothesis source, not an established architecture map for the chosen VLM.

### T59. [LMCap: Few-shot Multilingual Image Captioning by Retrieval Augmented Language Model Prompting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19821) (2023)

**Priority:** Start here. **Question links:** G7 primary; G9 context dependence.

**Method location:** Section 3: image-text retrieval and retrieval-augmented few-shot prompting. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.19821).

**Published method:** Retrieve captions with a multilingual vision-language encoder, supply them and demonstrations to an image-blind language model, then optionally rerank generated captions by image-text similarity.

**Original prerequisites:** A caption datastore and demonstration construction; its external stores and caption-quality evaluation are not imported.

**Bounded adaptation:** Released training images/captions and their category vectors can provide automatically formatted category-reporting examples. This is a fixed-ID reporting adaptation, not native-caption-quality evaluation.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Adds an explicit external-context route to S24/S04's accessibility/reporting comparison. S47/S23 require separating original-input features from retrieval-augmented ones.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Exclude every held-out image and all its language captions from retrieval and demonstrations. Retrieved labels belong to reference images, not the query. Declare retrieval model, context and supervision budgets.

**Limits / do not import:** Improvement can come from retrieved information or format demonstrations, not newly exposed internal knowledge. LMCap's decoder is image-blind; a VLM-based adaptation changes that architecture. Similarity reranking does not certify factuality.

### T60. [CONCAP: Seeing Beyond English with Concepts Retrieval-Augmented Captioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20411) (2025)

**Priority:** Start here. **Question links:** G7 primary; G9.

**Method location:** Sections 3.2-3.4: caption/concept retrieval; training setup. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.20411).

**Published method:** Combine retrieved captions and image-related concepts in a multilingual captioning prompt. The original system includes Q-Former/projection/LoRA training and generated language-specific concept templates checked through translation.

**Original prerequisites:** Its multilingual concept inventories, templates, translated training corpus and caption-evaluation targets are not supplied here.

**Bounded adaptation:** Use only a bounded analogue: information over the existing root-category identifiers, predicted without query gold, can accompany fixed-space reporting. Training-image references may be reused automatically; native lexical concept retrieval is not assumed.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Offers a distinct reporting-support route beyond S24/S05, while S33 separates the reliability of the supporting predictor from the reporter's correctness.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Keep oracle query labels out of prompts. Report whether supporting categories are predicted or retrieved, and track their errors. Separate additional evidence from a change to the original task.

**Limits / do not import:** This is not an unchanged CONCAP reproduction or training-free result from that paper. Category guidance may simply transfer another predictor's decisions; it does not prove faithful native-language reasoning or improved base-model knowledge.

### T05. [Word Translation Without Parallel Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04087) (2017)

**Priority:** Read when needed. **Question links:** Q6; G9; retrieval support for G7.

**Method location:** Sections 2.1-2.3: adversarial initialization, refinement and CSLS. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.04087).

**Published method:** Align word spaces without a seed bilingual dictionary using adversarial initialization and Procrustes refinement; use Cross-domain Similarity Local Scaling to reduce neighbor hubness.

**Original prerequisites:** The original unsupervised alignment assumes compatible word-space structures and evaluates against lexical correspondence, which the selected data does not provide.

**Bounded adaptation:** Retain density-corrected neighbor scoring over frozen training features as a candidate readout/retrieval ingredient. Unseeded alignment is conditional, not a default substitute for known image/category anchors.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Adds a retrieval-metric alternative to S34/S30; S39 and S33 prevent geometric matching or ranking scores from being mistaken for reliable category probabilities.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Declare the candidate pool, neighborhood fitting and target-input access. Do not let query gold or held-out-image counterparts choose the map or retrieval rule.

**Limits / do not import:** Indistinguishable language distributions need not preserve category identity. CSLS scores are not calibrated confidence, and the source's dictionary-free success does not establish identifiability on independent captions. No base-model adversarial editing is required.

### T06. [A robust self-learning method for fully unsupervised cross-lingual mappings of word embeddings](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1073/) (2018)

**Priority:** Read when needed. **Question links:** Q6; G9.

**Method location:** Section 3: normalization, structural initialization and robust self-learning. [Primary full text](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1073.pdf).

**Published method:** Initialize a cross-language mapping from structural similarities, then iteratively induce correspondences and refine mappings with normalization, stochastic self-learning and reweighting.

**Original prerequisites:** A structural comparability assumption and a meaningful induced-correspondence objective; it does not avoid all distributional assumptions merely by avoiding dictionaries.

**Bounded adaptation:** Normalization and iterative fitting are possible on released training features. Any inferred image/context matches are pseudo-correspondences, not verified translations or semantic labels.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Extends S30's feature-preprocessing choices and S34's constrained maps; S02/S44 require showing gains exceed extra fitting flexibility or memorization.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Keep initialization, component/match selection and iteration stopping inside declared training/development groups. Known image IDs and class labels must not be mislabeled as an unsupervised discovery.

**Limits / do not import:** A self-consistent mapping can still align wrong content or collapse useful distinctions. The full lexicon-induction task is outside scope. Image-based anchor mapping and unseeded pseudo-matching are different supervision settings.

### T23. [Choosing Transfer Languages for Cross-Lingual Learning](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1301/) (2019)

**Priority:** Read when needed. **Question links:** Q6; G9 source-selection component.

**Method location:** Transfer-language ranking formulation and feature-based ranker. [Primary full text](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1301.pdf).

**Published method:** Learn to rank source languages from observed cross-language task performance and dataset/linguistic features, instead of relying only on language-family intuition.

**Original prerequisites:** Its learned ranker uses many transfer observations and external linguistic features; four caption languages are not that meta-training resource.

**Bounded adaptation:** Retain the source-selection principle using declared development behavior and automatically available collection statistics. No new language-resource inventory or learned large-scale language ranker is assumed.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Complements S31/S39's transfer grids by treating source choice as selection, not an explanation. S33 makes confidence transfer a separate criterion.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Separate source-selection data from final target-image scoring. Selection using target labels is target-supervised development even without human work; balance source image/caption support.

**Limits / do not import:** The best source on this task is not a universal language ranking or proof of typological/cultural similarity. This ingredient does not reactivate standby Q11 as an independent study.

### T47. [MURAL: Multimodal, Multitask Representations Across Languages](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-emnlp.293/) (2021)

**Priority:** Read when needed. **Question links:** Q6; G9; modality/task separation for G7.

**Method location:** Section 3.1: weighted image-text/text-text objectives and task-specific projections. [Primary full text](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-emnlp.293.pdf).

**Published method:** Train shared text representations with image-text and text-translation contrastive objectives, using task-specific projection heads and explicit loss weighting.

**Original prerequisites:** The original large paired corpora and translation ranking supervision are outside the current data contract.

**Bounded adaptation:** Task-specific diagnostic projections could separate category-presence information from context alignment on frozen states. Only image/category correspondence is available; there is no translation objective to reproduce literally.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Adds a multi-objective/head-factorization idea to S17/S34. It challenges the assumption that one geometry must serve all tasks equally well, while S44 bounds extra capacity.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Expose which views, labels and losses train each head. Keep image-group separation, per-image weighting and direct category supervision as independent reference points.

**Limits / do not import:** A loss-weighted improvement does not prove stronger multilingual semantics. Identical category vectors do not make different images or captions semantically identical. No large-scale pretraining or model fine-tuning is a requirement of this adaptation.

### T49. [Cross-View Language Modeling: Towards Unified Cross-Lingual Cross-Modal Pre-training](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.315/) (2023)

**Priority:** Read when needed. **Question links:** Q6; G9; G7 context interpretation.

**Method location:** Section 3: cross-view contrastive, matching and conditional language modeling objectives. [Primary full text](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.315.pdf).

**Published method:** Unify image-text and parallel-text learning through shared architecture and contrastive, matching and conditional masked-language objectives.

**Original prerequisites:** The original fusion training uses both image-caption pairs and sentence translations; only the former correspondence is supplied by XM3600.

**Bounded adaptation:** The useful principle is declaring which view relation an objective preserves. A frozen-feature projection can target image identity or released category information, but those are distinct objectives and neither certifies full text equivalence.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Extends S34/S17 with objective choices and complements S16's task-relevance caution. It challenges calling all geometric agreement the same kind of semantic alignment.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** Do not make another caption of the same image an accidental contrastive negative. For category objectives, define positive/negative relations from known cells, including multilabel overlap and unknown masks.

**Limits / do not import:** No translation loss, semantic matching gold or full fusion-model training is imported. A contrastive objective or MI-bound motivation does not itself measure a causal visual-information quantity.

### T55. [Cross-lingual and Multilingual CLIP](https://aclanthology.org/2022.lrec-1.739/) (2022)

**Priority:** Read when needed. **Question links:** Q6; G9; G7 interpretation.

**Method location:** Section 3: cross-lingual teacher learning in a fixed visual space. [Primary full text](https://aclanthology.org/2022.lrec-1.739.pdf).

**Published method:** Keep the English CLIP teacher fixed and train another language encoder to match its embeddings on machine-translated text, then pair the student with the original visual encoder.

**Original prerequisites:** The original translation corpus and student-encoder training are not included in our frozen diagnostic scope.

**Bounded adaptation:** Preserve the fixed-reference idea for diagnostic feature/score mappings, using existing image/category anchors. Source-teacher and target-student roles must not imply that native captions are translations.

**Connection to / challenge for M1:** Makes S24's component interfaces and S34's readout geometry concrete. S29 and S33 challenge equating an English-aligned reference space with language-neutral or calibrated truth.

**Controls and fitting boundary:** State the teacher information available at fitting and inference; compare source references only within the same held-out-image policy. Keep feature imitation separate from category-label correctness.

**Limits / do not import:** Do not train a new language encoder or translate a corpus as an implicit prerequisite. Teacher agreement can reproduce source error or discard valid native content; its effect on reporting is not proof of better original category accessibility.

## 7. Cross-file synthesis for later preliminary-experiment design

The following are candidate methodological combinations. They do not certify novelty, select a model or specify experiments.

### 7.1 Q6: accessibility, coordinates and reference choice

M1 S01/S31 supplies category-readout and depth-dependent portability definitions; S34/S39 supplies constrained geometry and direction comparisons. M2 T02/T04/T14 adds explicit map and normalization alternatives. Together these make it possible to distinguish several operational measurements: unchanged source-head transfer, target-context category accessibility, input-statistic adjustment and fitted feature mapping. Their relative values are evidence about those procedures, not a unique diagnosis of why the VLM fails.

T18/T55 challenges treating English as a neutral reference: forcing a native caption state to reproduce an English caption state can remove genuine content differences. A common category target avoids requiring full sentence equivalence, but also narrows the represented meaning. T47/T49 supplies the principle that different view/task objectives can use different projections. Neither approach proves preservation of all native content.

### 7.2 G9: keep discrimination, similarity and reliability distinct

M1 S33 defines correctness confidence; S39 separates geometric alignment from useful prediction. M2 adds transformations and retrieval metrics whose scores may be on completely different scales. A map can change margins or rank neighbors without making probabilities reliable. Cosine similarity, CSLS, inverted softmax and contrastive temperature do not automatically estimate the probability that a category decision is correct.

Teacher-generated scores are estimates, not semantic truth. A presence head and a correctness head have distinct labels; correctness training requires held-out or cross-fitted underlying decisions as described in M1. Hard existing CSV targets remain the assessment reference. Target-fitted temperature/threshold/mapping changes remain target adaptation even when all labels were already released.

### 7.3 G7: reporting support versus original-input accessibility

M1 S24/S04/S05 distinguishes accessible category information, position and generated reporting. M2 T59/T60 adds routes that supply training examples or category guidance through the prompt. This can be useful as a reporting-related diagnostic ingredient, but the extra information changes the task input. An improved output may copy a retrieved label pattern, imitate a supporting predictor, gain format practice or benefit from additional context; it is not automatically recovery of knowledge independently present in the original VLM state.

The permissible adaptation is fixed-ID reporting with the same released root labels. It is not evaluation of generated Indic captions, translation fidelity or a native concept lexicon. Individual versus joint reporting must preserve the distinction among category errors, format errors and the arithmetic difficulty of making an entire set correct. Existing category multiplicity is not instance count.

### 7.4 Source choice is a selection issue, not yet an explanation

M1 S31/S39 makes source-target asymmetry measurable. T23/T27 emphasizes declaring which source/target observations choose a source, layer or adaptation. Automatically available category performance can guide a development choice, but it does not prove that typological distance, culture or pretraining exposure caused the result. Standby Q11 remains standby as an independent question.

## 8. Non-negotiable assumptions for any adopted ingredient

### 8.1 Three correspondences that must not be conflated

| Relation available or proposed | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Two caption records share an image ID | They describe the same released image; image-presence targets are shared | Identical asserted entities, equivalent wording, translation fidelity or equal semantic content |
| Two samples share a category bit/vector | The released coarse presence labels coincide on those known cells | Identical images, object instances, fine subtypes or full caption meaning |
| A learned map or neighbor rule matches two feature vectors | The fitting criterion or retrieval score favors that pairing | Verified translation, native category-name equivalence, causal mechanism or truthful generated text |

Mapping authors' dictionaries/parallel pairs onto same-image anchors therefore changes the original assumption. We can test an operational category-readout relation, but cannot validate lexical or sentence-level equivalence without the extra semantic work the user excluded.

### 8.2 Supervision and information-access ledger

| Setting | Permitted data use | Correct interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Unchanged source readout | Source training labels/features and source-only selection; held-out target inputs for scoring | Source-only probe transfer under the chosen input conditions |
| Target-input statistical mapping | Declared target training/development inputs and/or released image pair identities, without semantic target labels | Input-informed or paired-image adaptation; image IDs are correspondence supervision |
| Target-label fitting/selection | Released target-context presence labels on training/development images | Target-supervised diagnostic adaptation/comparator; no new human work, but not unchanged-source zero-shot transfer |
| Retrieval/in-context support | Training-only references and their released labels; no held-out image counterpart | Added context and supervision budget; not the original-input task |
| Held-out target test label access for fitting | Not permitted | Evaluation leakage, regardless of whether labels were publicly released |

Input-only fitting that uses the eventual test distribution is transductive and must be explicitly declared if considered. It is not equivalent to fitting solely on training images. Numerical development selection remains allowed; new human verification does not.

### 8.3 Map direction, anchor support and dimensionality

The feature dimension can exceed the number of independent training-image anchors. Repeated captions increase observations but not independent visual identities. An SVD-based orthogonal solution can exist while its behavior in unsupported directions is underdetermined. Regularized or reduced-rank maps are different estimators with different capacity, not automatic proof that the spaces are globally isomorphic.

Fit means, norms, PCA bases, dimensions and mapping constraints inside declared training/development groups. State whether the map goes source-to-target or target-to-source and how the category head is applied; reversing a non-orthogonal map is not innocuous. If direction/subspace comparisons span transformed spaces, express them in a common basis or identify the comparison as a new coordinate system. M1's S39 coefficient-scaling correction still matters.

Observed mean differences can reflect caption content, selection, tokenization or pooling as well as language. Subtracting a mean does not validate it as a pure language component. A mapping fitted to category labels can learn the task; its success is not proof that it merely repaired coordinates. Identity/normalization-only references and comparable readout capacity are relevant controls, not new semantic data.

### 8.4 Contrastive targets and pseudo-correspondence

If a view-level objective is considered, multiple captions of the same image are not unrelated negatives. If an objective instead operates on category labels, a shared positive root establishes only that root's target; two images with overlapping categories may differ in every other relevant detail. Unknown cells do not become negatives. Different objective granularities cannot share the same “semantic alignment” claim without qualification.

Induced neighbor pairs, teacher features and contrastive pseudo-labels are model-dependent estimates. They cannot replace missing independent caption-assertion or translation gold. Fully unseeded mappings can be unidentifiable or match the wrong content even when distributions align. This is why T05/T06 are conditional ingredients, not a guarantee that additional human verification has become unnecessary for the original lexicon task.

### 8.5 Retrieval, context and category guidance

The retrieval index, demonstrations and supporting-predictor fitting must exclude **all captions and language views of every held-out image**. Retrieving another language's caption for the same test image is still identity leakage. A training reference's category vector is allowed reference information; the query's category vector is evaluation gold and cannot be supplied as guidance. The number/language of references and context/token budget are part of the method definition.

At inference, query information may include the image, its permitted original caption context and computed features; it cannot include a gold-conditioned category selection. A predicted category cue must be named as a prediction with its own error profile. If a reporter repeats that prediction, the system may simply inherit the predictor's behavior. A ground-truth-guided oracle, if ever separately defined, cannot be presented as a deployable method or used to substantiate base-model knowledge.

External pretrained encoders can affect retrieval quality and information access. Their contribution is separate from that of the VLM being probed. Teacher/retriever/target-language gains require scoring against the existing labels; similarity itself cannot certify truthful content. Before/after-retrieval internal states differ in their inputs and cannot be equated as the same evidence budget.

### 8.6 Statistics and supported scope

Reuse M1's image-group partitions and grouped uncertainty calculations across all four languages. Preserve sparse-category support, unknown masks, per-image weighting and separate discrimination/calibration/reporting outcomes. Do not select the best layer, mapping, source, neighborhood size, retrieval bank or prompt arrangement on test labels. A larger search space and added context need fair accounting.

The resulting claims concern a frozen model and this collection's observed native-caption contexts. Neither M1 nor M2 can identify a pure language effect, native instruction comprehension, cultural mismatch or fine-grained visual hallucination from the shared category labels alone. Automatically derivable tasks are feasible within that bound; they do not remove the bound.

## 9. Handoff for preliminary-experiment design

The next design can jointly select a compact subset of M1's diagnostic measurements and M2's transfer/reporting ingredients. For each candidate, the needed record is: ongoing question, exact target, observed state/output, correspondence assumption, fitting inputs/labels, additional context, controls, computability from released files and maximum supported claim. The method pack should stay small enough that changes in capacity, supervision and input information remain interpretable.

This file does not choose an experiment grid, prescribe a training run, generate new prompts/corpora, finalize a competing explanation or certify novelty. Many ideas overlap, and combining known techniques is not by itself a research contribution. The full method combination and closest-prior-work comparison remain decisions for that later stage.
