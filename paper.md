# Research papers: probing, transfer and multilingual VLM evaluation

Updated: 13 November 2026.

146 unique papers. Category 1: 38 probing references; category 2: 62 cross-language transfer references; category 3: 15 intersection references; category 4: 42 multilingual VLM benchmark/dataset/evaluation references. Category 4 cross-lists 11 existing references, each counted once in the unique total.

**Scope.** A reading list for assessing candidate probing questions and methodological inspiration across text-only language models and vision-language models. Cross-language transfer covers languages and language families globally, including different scripts and resource levels; it is not restricted to Indic languages. Foundational work, method papers, surveys, and benchmarks explicitly designed to evaluate transfer are included.

**Earlier question-relevance review.** All 115 references in categories 1–3 were checked against the then-current broader candidate questions. No additional papers were removed at that stage: each had a credible methodological, conceptual or interpretive contribution under that conservative criterion. **Questions** tags below record those earlier links, not novelty or an experimental commitment. The active shortlist in [research.md](research.md) has since been restricted to questions requiring no new manual annotation or human verification; some tagged question IDs are now retired. This bibliography has not been re-pruned against that smaller shortlist. Category 4 is unchanged. The earlier check used recorded paper contributions and primary-source follow-up for borderline entries, rather than a new full-paper review of all 115 references.

**Origin.** **[Plan]** marks a paper cited in your supplied experiment plan. All unmarked papers are additions found for this expanded list. Each entry has a paper link and a short reason for inclusion. Years follow the linked publication; arXiv entries use the first preprint year.

**VLM coverage.** **[VLM]** marks vision-language-specific papers, including multilingual encoders, generative VLMs and task-specific evaluation resources. On 13 November, a targeted primary-source literature audit added multilingual hallucination work (including MHR and CLAIM), encoder diagnosis, spatial and hallucination probes, multilingual pretraining, retrieval and captioning. Both multilingual-VLM and modality-collapse surveys were included as further reading. This is a curated bibliography, not an exhaustive systematic review or a claim that every relevant paper has been found. Inclusion and placement were checked from paper abstracts, method descriptions and proceedings metadata; it is not a full-paper review of every entry.

**Classification.** Categories 1–3 preserve their previous primary placements. Category 1 is restricted to non-interventional diagnostic readouts, lenses and probing methodology. Cross-language transfer covers methods and evaluations of generalization between languages. The intersection includes either actual cross-language probe transfer or probing that explicitly investigates the representations underlying cross-language transfer; these are distinguished in the entry descriptions. Multilingual probing alone does not automatically imply cross-language transfer. Category 4 is a benchmark/dataset/evaluation reading index and explicitly marks papers already listed in another category.

## 1. Probing (38)

Non-interventional diagnostic readouts and probing methodology relevant to XM3600 object-category representations and language-conditioned captioning.

**Research use.** Use the shared, human-annotated 25 image-category labels as object-presence targets across languages. Caption mention is a separate target requiring a reliable mention annotation or extraction procedure. Readouts from hidden states, pooled features or logits can test accessibility; generated-caption comparisons are behavioral evaluation. Extracting activations is data collection, while fitting or applying a diagnostic readout is probing.

**Selection boundary.** This section retains layer-wise and cross-position readouts, linear/nonlinear diagnostic classifiers, logit/tuned lenses, probe capacity and selectivity controls, information-theoretic measures, and probe generalization. Intervention-centered papers and peripheral task-specific studies were removed. For mixed-method papers retained here, only their probing analyses are in scope; their causal edits, steering or other interventions are not recommendations for this study. Probe decodability alone does not show that the model uses the decoded information.

## 2. Cross-language transfer (62)

Methods, source-language selection, alignment, and evaluations of transferring capabilities between languages, spanning text and vision-language tasks.

**Current research setting.** XM3600 plus the [image-level object annotations](https://github.com/uriberger/cross_lingual_diff_in_descs/blob/main/data/xm3600_annotation.csv) from [Cross-Lingual and Cross-Cultural Variation in Image Descriptions](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.478/). The CSV has 3,600 image rows and 25 root-synset category columns. It supplies image-content annotations; caption mention frequencies measure a different quantity. A present category need not be mentioned in every valid caption, and fine-grained object absence cannot be inferred from these coarse labels.

**Dataset interpretation.** The [repository README](https://github.com/uriberger/cross_lingual_diff_in_descs#readme) warns against its derived Bengali/Telugu mention datasets because its English-translation stage is unreliable for those languages. This warning concerns the derived extraction pipeline, not the original human-written XM3600 captions or the language-independent CSV annotations. Language coverage and mention-extraction validity must be checked before selecting Indic arms.

**Earlier dataset-focused selection.** Four peripheral benchmark references were previously removed from this category: XNLI, XTREME, HinTel-AlignBench, and MMCRICBENCH. Papers with credible ideas for alignment, source selection, distillation, calibration, language-sensitive content selection or captioning were retained, including text-only methods. XTREME-R and IndicBERT/IndicXTREME remain for diagnostic retrieval/evaluation ideas and language-capability controls. The question-relevance review above makes no additional deletions.

**Reading priority.** **[Priority]** marks twelve previously selected starting points for technique inspiration; the remaining papers are supporting references. Question links record relevance to the earlier, broader candidate set. They do not establish that an adaptation is novel or select a final research question.

## 3. Intersection: Probing + Cross-language transfer (15)

Non-interventional probing methods that transfer across languages, or diagnose the representations underlying such transfer, with credible applications to the XM3600 object-label study.

**Research use and selection.** Retain methods for cross-language diagnostic readouts, validation-only layer/source selection, feature selection within a probe, structural geometry, lexical alignment and lens calibration. Object-presence labels remain the same image-level human annotations across languages; presence and caption mention are separate targets. Intervention-centered studies and adaptation-first work without a distinctive needed probe method were removed in the earlier review. Question links below refer to provisional research questions, not established findings or novelty claims.

The scope here excludes activation patching, erasure, steering and edits to the base model during inference. ## 4. Multilingual VLM benchmarks, datasets and evaluation (42)

Papers introducing, extending, analyzing or evaluating vision-language datasets and benchmarks across languages, including non-English language-specific extensions and multilingual evaluator studies. Coverage is global, not restricted to Indic languages.

**Purpose.** Read this category to map evaluation practices and reported limitations before selecting research questions. Distinguish translated fixed-image tests, independently authored native captions/questions, cultural sampling, image-text language mismatch, script/dialect variation, answer extraction and judge bias. These distinctions help formulate candidate questions; inclusion does not establish an open research gap or guarantee novelty.

**XM3600 starting points.** **[Start here]** marks nine closely relevant foundations and evaluation references. In your selected data, the 25 human-annotated object-category labels apply per image and are shared across languages; caption mention rates are a separate, language-dependent observation. A difference in valid human captions is not automatically a model error.

**Scope and cross-listing.** This section includes language-specific VLM evaluation outside English as well as multilingual studies. Entries summarize the benchmark contribution, language coverage and a reading focus. Papers that also propose models or interventions are included here only for their benchmark/evaluation component; this does not restore intervention methods to categories 1 or 3. Eleven entries are cross-listed from category 2 and do not count as new unique papers. The source checks cover primary abstracts, proceedings metadata and relevant methodology descriptions, rather than a full systematic review of every paper.
